## What kind of article is this?

The publisher labels the source a **Review Article**. Its actual contribution is a conceptual and prescriptive framework for working with AI. It gathers definitions, analogies, workplace observations, and advice into seven pillars. It does not describe a systematic-review search, inclusion criteria, evidence grading, or a new empirical study. This matters because an appealing organizing framework and a validated intervention answer different questions. [PDF p. 1](https://biomedgrid.com/pdf/AJBSR.MS.ID.003878.pdf#page=1)

The abstract contrasts retrieving stored information with using AI to produce designs and support automation. The author worries that adoption alone can fail to improve thinking or create value. His proposed response is to develop human habits of inquiry, purposeful change, broad judgment, and responsibility. Statements about neural engagement and cognitive laziness in the abstract are not accompanied by a new cognitive experiment in this paper. Treat them as motivating assertions requiring evidence beyond the article.

## Four workforce scenarios

The introduction attributes a four-scenario framing to reference [1]. It sketches: technical progress with an unprepared workforce; extensive automation with skills left behind; stalled adoption despite technical development; and a gradual transition in which people and AI collaborate. The fourth is the author's preferred direction. [Introduction, PDF p. 1](https://biomedgrid.com/pdf/AJBSR.MS.ID.003878.pdf#page=1)

These are scenario descriptions, not forecasts with assigned probabilities. The paper does not estimate how many organizations occupy each scenario or compare outcomes across them. A productive reading question is therefore: **what would have to change for a particular workplace to move toward the collaborative scenario?** The pillars are the author's proposed response to that question.

## How the framework fits together

The conclusion groups the argument into a foundation of curiosity and purposeful adaptation, a strategic layer of broad systems thinking and outcome selection, and an action layer of collaboration, stewardship, and creation. This is an interpretive organization of the article's own closing discussion, not a measured causal sequence. [Conclusions, PDF pp. 4-5](https://biomedgrid.com/pdf/AJBSR.MS.ID.003878.pdf#page=4)

Figure 1 on PDF p. 2 depicts a temple whose seven columns support an AI-era mindset. Its visual message is mutual support: a narrow focus on one habit misses the larger structure. It contains no data series, quantities, causal estimates, or evaluation results. The original figure is not redistributed here.

## Glossary

| Term | Working meaning in this companion |
| --- | --- |
| Mindset | Beliefs and habits that shape how someone interprets a situation and acts within it; the article's organizing concept. |
| Purpose | The lasting value or direction a change is intended to serve. |
| Outcome | The condition one wants to change, distinguished from merely producing more outputs. |
| Open system | A chosen task and the surrounding people, institutions, constraints, and feedback that can affect it. |
| Virtual teammate | A management metaphor for assigning AI tasks, supplying context, questioning output, and integrating work. It does not grant AI moral agency. |
| Stewardship | Responsibility for broader and longer-term consequences of the system one builds or deploys. |
| Builder's mindset | The drive to create a new arrangement using the other pillars, rather than only improve an existing task. |
| Validation | Evidence from a specified study capable of supporting a claim; not supplied by a diagram or recommendation alone. |

The article uses the unusual phrase “Think of Wide-Open on Completed Systems.” This guide preserves that label for source navigation and explains it as broad systems thinking. It does not silently replace the article with a more formal systems theory.

## Check your understanding

Explain why “our staff uses AI every day” does not, on its own, establish that staff learn more, make better decisions, or create more value. Then identify one of the four scenarios that could describe your example and state what additional evidence would distinguish it from the others. This is a companion exercise, not an assessment instrument from the paper.
